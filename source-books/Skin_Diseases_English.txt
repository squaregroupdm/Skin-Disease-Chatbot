1. Impetigo
========================================
Impetigo is a highly contagious bacterial skin infection caused by Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes. It mainly affects children, but can also occur in adults. Impetigo most often occurs around the nose, mouth, cheeks or on the extremities. It is basically of 2 types: non-bullous (most common) and bullous.

Signs & Symptoms:
Non-bullous impetigo:
- The sores grow into blisters, then burst leaving thick, yellowish crusts
- The crusts leave red marks that fade without leaving scars
Bullous impetigo:
- Symptoms begin with the appearance of fluid-filled blisters (bullae) that occur between waist & neck or on arms & legs
- The blisters may quickly spread, before bursting to leave a yellow crust that usually heals without leaving any scars
- The blisters may be itching and painful
- Fever and swollen glands are more common

Treatment:
- Topical antibiotics like Mupirocin, Fusidic Acid or Retapamulin
- Oral antibiotics like Amoxicillin/Clavulanate, Cephalexin, Clindamycin etc.

Recommended products: Retabac Ointment (Retapamulin), Bactrocin Ointment (Mupirocin), Fusitop-HC Cream (Fusidic Acid & Hydrocortisone Acetate)

2. Tinea Pedis (Athlete's Foot)
========================================
It is a foot infection due to a dermatophyte fungus. Most frequently it occurs due to Trichophyton rubrum.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Patchy fine dry scaling on the sole of the foot
- Clusters of blisters or pustules on the sides of the feet
- Round dry patches on the top of the foot

Treatment:
- Usually treated with topical antifungal agents
- If the treatment is unsuccessful, oral antifungal medicines may be considered, including Terbinafine and Itraconazole

Recommended products: Xfin Cream (Terbinafine Hydrochloride), Lulitop Cream (Luliconazole)

========================================
3. Tinea Corporis (Ringworm)
========================================
Tinea corporis or ringworm of the body, is a common, contagious superficial fungal infection causing itchy, red, ring-shaped patches with scaly, raised borders. Caused by dermatophytes, it spreads via direct skin contact with humans/animals or contaminated items.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Annular Lesions: A classic ring-shaped rash with a scaly, raised border and central clearing
- Itching: The rash is typically pruritic (itchy)
- Appearance: Red, scaly or cracked skin

Treatment:
- Usually treated with topical antifungal agents
- Sometimes Hydrocortisone/Triamcinolone Acetonide is added for faster relief of itch
- If the treatment is unsuccessful, oral antifungal medicines may be considered including Terbinafine and Itraconazole

Recommended products: Xfin Cream (Terbinafine Hydrochloride), Lulitop Cream (Luliconazole), Clopirox Cream (Ciclopirox Olamine)

========================================
4. Tinea Cruris
========================================
It is a dermatophyte fungal infection of the groin region (more often seen in males).

Signs & Symptoms:
- The rash has a scaly raised red border that spreads down the inner thighs from the groin or scrotum
- May form ring-like patterns on the buttocks
- Can be very itchy

Treatment:
- Usually treated with topical antifungal agents
- Sometimes Hydrocortisone is added for faster relief of itch
- If the treatment is unsuccessful, oral antifungal medicines may be considered including Terbinafine and Itraconazole

Recommended products: Pevitin Cream (Econazole Nitrate + Triamcinolone Acetonide), Betameson-CL Cream (Clotrimazole + Betamethasone Dipropionate), Fungidal-HC Cream (Miconazole Nitrate + Hydrocortisone Acetate)

========================================
5. Tinea Capitis
========================================
It is a fungal infection that affects the scalp.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Dry scaling – like dandruff but usually with hair loss
- Black dots – the hairs are broken off at the scalp surface, which is scaly
- Smooth areas of hair loss
- Kerion – very inflamed mass, like an abscess
- Favus – yellow crusts and matted hair

Treatment:
- Topical antifungal agents
- Oral antifungal medicines including Terbinafine and Itraconazole

Recommended products: Xfin Cream (Terbinafine Hydrochloride), Oxifun Cream & Lotion (Oxiconazole)

========================================
6. Tinea Faciei
========================================
It is a superficial dermatophyte infection limited to the glabrous skin of the face.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Round or oval red scaly patches
- Abscess

Treatment:
- Usually treated with topical antifungal agents
- If the treatment is unsuccessful, oral antifungal medicines may be considered

Recommended products: Fungidal-HC Cream (Miconazole Nitrate + Hydrocortisone Acetate), Fungidal Cream (Miconazole Nitrate)

========================================
7. Tinea Manuum
========================================
It is a fungal infection of the hand. It is typically more aggressive than Tinea Pedis but similar in look.

Signs & Symptoms:
- The area infected with tinea will be itchy, red, and have a scaly appearance
- The infected area may also peel and flake
- The infection will generally start on the palm of the hand and may or may not spread to fingers and the back of hand

Treatment:
- Anti-fungal creams, lotions and oral preparations

Recommended products: Lulitop Cream (Luliconazole), Clopirox Cream (Ciclopirox Olamine)

========================================
8. Onychomycosis
========================================
Onychomycosis is a common fungal infection of the nail plate, bed, or matrix, causing discolored, thick, and brittle nails, often affecting toenails more than fingernails.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Discoloration: Nails turn white, yellow or brown
- Thickening: Nails become thick, brittle or ragged
- Separation: The nail may separate from the nail bed (Onycholysis)
- Shape Changes: Nails can become distorted or misshapen
- Debris: Accumulation of crumbling material under the nail

Treatment:
- Oral Antifungals: Commonly, Terbinafine or Itraconazole are considered the most effective for severe cases
- Topical Treatment: Medicated nail lacquers like Amorolfine or Ciclopirox or creams for mild infections
- Combined Therapy: Combining oral and topical treatments can improve cure rates
- Laser Therapy: An alternative or complementary treatment to heat the fungal cells, often used in conjunction with debridement
- Nail Removal: In severe cases, removing the nail for direct application of treatment to the nail bed

Recommended products: Xfin Cream (Terbinafine Hydrochloride)

9. Tinea Versicolor
========================================
It is a common condition that causes small patches of skin which become scaly and discoloured.

Signs & Symptoms:
Spots on the skin. These spots may:
- Be lighter (or darker) than surrounding skin. The color of the spots can be white, pink, salmon, red, tan or brown
- Appear anywhere on the body
- Cause the affected skin to itch

Treatment:
- Antifungal creams, lotions and oral preparations

Recommended products: Xfin Cream (Terbinafine Hydrochloride), Afun Cream (Clotrimazole)

10. Scabies
========================================
Scabies is a contagious skin infestation by the mite Sarcoptes scabiei. When first infected, usually two to six weeks are required before symptoms occur. If a person develops a second infection later in life, symptoms may begin within a day. These symptoms can be present across most of the body or just certain areas such as the wrists, between fingers, or along the waist-line. The head may be affected, but this is typically only in young children. The itch is often worse at night.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Intense itching
- Pimple-like rash

Treatment:
- Topical Permethrin
- Oral Ivermectin
- Topical Crotamiton

Recommended products: Scabex Cream (Permethrin), Alice Tablet (Ivermectin)

11. Atopic Eczema — Eczema (Dermatitis)
========================================
Dermatitis or Eczema is an inflammatory disorder of the skin which results in varying degrees of malfunction of the epidermis. It usually develops an itchy red rash that initially weeps or oozes serum and may become crusted, thickened or scaly. Eczematous rash may result from various causes including allergies, irritating chemicals, drugs, scratching or rubbing the skin or sun exposure. The rash may become secondarily infected by bacteria, fungus etc.

Atopic Eczema: A chronic pruritic inflammatory disorder affecting the epidermis, commencing in infancy, often persisting throughout childhood but eventually remitting and sometimes recurring in adult life.

Signs & Symptoms:
- An itching rash
- Patches
- Vesicles
- Lichenification (effect of scratching/rubbing)
- Weeping

Treatment:
- Emollients
- Topical steroids
- Antihistamines
- Anti-infectives
- Tacrolimus

Recommended products: Topicort Cream (Hydrocortisone Acetate), Remus Ointment (Tacrolimus 0.03%)

12. Seborrheic Eczema
========================================
An acute or subacute inflammatory skin disease of unknown cause, beginning on the scalp and characterized by rounded, irregular or circular lesions covered with yellow or brown-gray greasy scales.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Pink/red patches
- Scaly surface

Treatment:
- Topical steroids (alone or with Keratolytic agents)

Recommended products: Dermasol-S Scalp Solution (Clobetasol Propionate), Prosalic Lotion (Betamethasone + Salicylic Acid)

13. Discoid (Nummular) Eczema
========================================
Eczema with coin or oval shaped lesions. It is often associated with dry skin & worsens in dry weather. Discoid or nummular dermatitis often appears after a skin injury, burn, abrasion or insect bite. Men are affected more than women. Men often have first outbreak between 55 & 65 years of age. But women are usually affected when they are teenagers or young adults.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Most noticeable symptom is a patch of coin shaped lesion
- The lesions frequently develop on the arms & legs; but may eventually spread to the torso & hands
- May be brown, pink or red
- Some lesions may itch & burn
- Some lesions may ooze fluid & eventually crust over

Treatment:
- Potent or super potent steroids

Recommended products: Halobet Cream & Ointment (Halobetasol Propionate), Dermasol Cream & Ointment (Clobetasol Propionate)

14. Pompholyx (Dyshidrotic Eczema)
========================================
A blistering itchy rash of the hands and feet, marked by episodic and recurring deep-seated vesicles or bullae. The rash is most often found in adolescents and young adults (specially during spring & summer).

Signs & Symptoms:
- Very itchy 'bubbles' under the skin of the palmar or plantar surfaces which weep and then dry and become painful and cracked
- Lesions appear as tiny vesicles which may coalesce and become bullae, then break and ooze serous fluid and subsequently dry when the skin is red, dry hyperkeratotic and fissured

Treatment:
Medication for acute attack:
- Systemic steroids
- Soaks (Aluminium Acetate or Potassium Permanganate)
- Potent or super potent steroids
Medication for chronic attack:
- Potent or super potent steroids
- Emollients

Recommended products: Halobet Cream & Ointment (Halobetasol Propionate), Emolent Cream (Light liquid paraffin & White soft paraffin)

15. Contact Dermatitis
========================================
An eczematous response of the skin due to contact with either an external irritant (primary irritant dermatitis) or allergen (allergic contact dermatitis). The former is a result of cumulative exposure to an irritant substance.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Intense itching
- Erythema, local edema and blister formation
- The blisters may weep in severe cases

Treatment:
- Topical steroids of appropriate strengths

Recommended products: Ezex Cream & Ointment (Clobetasone Butyrate), Topicort Cream (Hydrocortisone Acetate)

========================================
16. Varicose Eczema
========================================
An inflammatory disorder of the skin of the lower legs associated with venous hypertension. This condition usually happens due to impaired return of blood from the legs. It mainly occurs in the elderly.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Affected skin becomes itchy, red, swollen, dry and flaky, scaly or crusty
- Some may experience brown discoloration of the skin
- Sometimes red, tender and tight skin that can eventually get hardened
- Pain may occur in some patients

Treatment:
- Self-help measures to improve blood circulation (avoiding long time standing or sitting, frequently raising the legs)
- Regular use of emollients
- Topical steroids
- Antibiotics (if infected)

Recommended products: Dermasol Cream & Ointment (Clobetasol Propionate), Equra Cream (Urea)

17. Lichen Simplex Chronicus
========================================
It is a localized, well-circumscribed area of lichenification (thickened skin) resulting from repeated rubbing, itching and scratching of the skin. It can occur on normal skin of individuals with Atopic, Seborrheic and Contact Dermatitis or Psoriasis.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Pruritus or intense itching
- A solitary plaque of lichen simplex
- Linear or oval in shape and markedly thickened
- Dry or scaly surface
- Exaggerated skin markings or scratch marks

Treatment:
- Super potent steroids

Recommended products: Dermasol Cream & Ointment (Clobetasol Propionate), Halobet Cream & Ointment (Halobetasol Propionate)

18. Lichen Planus
========================================
An inflammatory rash marked by the presence of itchy, distinctive papules, which typically appear on the scalp, in the oral cavity or on the limbs. Typically, the rash persists for 1 to 2 years and then spontaneously improves. But recurrence may occur in some patients.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Very pruritic rash
- Red to violet, polygon-shaped papules
- The papules may merge into plaques crisscrossed by faint lines

Treatment:
- Super potent topical steroids
- Systemic corticosteroids taken orally

Recommended products: Dermasol Cream & Ointment (Clobetasol Propionate), Halobet Cream & Ointment (Halobetasol Propionate)

19. Insect Bites
========================================
An itchy urticarial response to a biting or stinging Arthropod, which sometimes acts to transmit diseases such as Malaria (mosquitoes), Leishmaniasis (sand flies) and Rocky mountain spotted fever (ticks).

Signs & Symptoms:
- Itchy spots
- Red, urticated papules or wheals, occasionally vesicles or bullae often with a red central punctum or excoriated surface

Treatment:
- Elimination of the source
- Calamine
- Crotamiton/Hydrocortisone in combination
- Systemic & topical antihistamines
- Systemic antibiotics (if secondary infection is present)

Recommended products: Togent Cream (Diphenhydramine HCl & Zinc Acetate)

20. Psoriasis
========================================
A chronic disorder in which red, scaly plaques with sharply defined borders appear on the skin. The rash is commonly found on the knees, shins, elbows, lower back, buttocks and along the hairline. The most common age of onset is 10-40 years. Although the cause of psoriasis is unknown, emotional stress, skin trauma, cold weather, infections and some drugs may trigger attacks.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Lesions: Papules with well-defined plaques
- Colour: Red color may result in post-inflammatory hyper or hypo-pigmentation
- Surface: Thick, easily detached white silver scale with pin-point bleeding points underneath
- Shape: Various, including round and annular

Treatment:
Topical:
- Vitamin D3 analogues
- Tar
- Vitamin A
- Potent/super potent topical steroids
- Dithranol
- Ultraviolet light (very useful, especially narrow band at 310 nm)
Systemic:
- Methotrexate
- Photochemotherapy (PUVA)
- Retinoids
- Cyclosporine A

Recommended products: Dyvon Ointment (Calcipotriol), Dyvon Plus Ointment & Topical Suspension (Betamethasone Dipropionate + Calcipotriol), Ezex Cream & Ointment (Clobetasone Butyrate)

21. Scalp Psoriasis
========================================
A chronic inflammatory skin condition affecting the scalp, characterized by well-defined, red plaques covered with silvery-white scales. It results from an accelerated skin cell turnover and may extend beyond the hairline. It can occur at any age and often follows a relapsing course.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Well-defined, thick, scaly plaques on the scalp
- Persistent itching and irritation
- Flaking resembling severe dandruff
- Redness that may extend to forehead, neck or behind the ears
- In some cases, cracking or mild bleeding

Treatment:
- Topical corticosteroids
- Vitamin D analogues
- Combination therapy with corticosteroid for better efficacy
- Regular scalp care and avoidance of triggers

Recommended products: Prosalic Lotion (Betamethasone + Salicylic Acid), Dermasol-S Scalp Solution (Clobetasol Propionate), Dyvon Plus Topical Suspension (Betamethasone Dipropionate + Calcipotriol)

22. Melasma
========================================
A patchy brown hyperpigmentation of face (especially the forehead, cheeks, upper lip, chin and jaw) due to solar damage. It is more common in women who tan easily and during pregnancy.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Patchy, flat, brown pigmentation
- An unsightly discoloration on the face, which is darkened by sunlight

Treatment:
- Complete avoidance of ultraviolet radiation
- Sunblocks
- Anti-melasma cream

Recommended products: Melano Cream (Fluocinolone Acetonide 0.01%, Hydroquinone 4% & Tretinoin 0.05%), Sunmask Cream (Zinc Oxide, Avobenzone, Octinoxate & Enzacamene)

23. Acne
========================================
A chronic disorder of the pilosebaceous apparatus associated with excess production of sebum, bacterial overgrowth (particularly of Cutibacterium acnes) and blockage of the pilosebaceous duct resulting in a variety of inflammatory manifestations on the face and torso.

Signs & Symptoms:
Spot under the skin which may be itchy or painful.
- Comedones (blackheads)
- Whiteheads
- Papules
- Pustules
- Nodules
- Cysts
- Scars
- Keloids
- Hyperpigmented macules

Treatment:
Topical:
- Antibacterials
- Keratolytics
- Retinoic acid
Systemic:
- Antibiotics
- Anti-androgens (for females only)
- Isotretinoin

Recommended products: Fona Plus Gel (Adapalene + Benzoyl Peroxide), Clinface Gel (Clindamycin Phosphate & Tretinoin)

24. Warts
========================================
Warts are benign (not cancerous) skin growths that appear when a virus infects the top layer of the skin. Viruses that cause warts are called human papilloma virus (HPV). Wart viruses are contagious. Warts can spread by contact with the wart or something that touched the wart.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Warts are often skin-colored and feel rough, but they can be dark (brown or gray-black), flat, and smooth
- Can have black dots that look like seeds
- Most often feel like rough bumps

Treatment:
- Weak Acids in various concentrations
- Cryotherapy
- Electrosurgery and curettage
- Excision
- Laser treatment
- Immunotherapy

Recommended products: Salicid Cream (Salicylic Acid)

25. Corns and Calluses
========================================
Corns and calluses are thick, hardened layers of skin that develop when skin tries to protect itself against friction and pressure. They most often develop on the feet and toes or hands and fingers. Corns and calluses are not the same thing. Corns have a hard center and tend to develop on the tops and sides of toes. They can be painful. Calluses usually develop on the soles of the feet and palms of the hands. They can vary in size and shape and are rarely painful.

Signs & Symptoms:
- A thick, rough area of skin
- A hardened, raised bump
- Tenderness or pain under skin
- Flaky, dry or waxy skin

Treatment:
- Weak Acids in various concentrations
- Cryotherapy
- Electrosurgery and curettage
- Excision
- Laser treatment
- Immunotherapy

Recommended products: Salicid Cream (Salicylic Acid)

26. Alopecia
========================================
Alopecia is a hair-loss condition which usually affects the scalp. It causes one or more patches of hair loss and affects both genders. It is an autoimmune disorder, in which the immune system attacks hair follicles. For most patients, the condition resolves without treatment within a year, but the hair loss sometimes becomes permanent.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Gradual thinning on top of head
- Circular or patchy bald spots
- Sudden loosening of hair
- Patches of scaling that spread over the scalp

Treatment:
- Topical Minoxidil
- Immunotherapy
- Steroidal treatment

Recommended products: Splendora Topical Solution (Minoxidil)

27. Vitiligo
========================================
It causes the skin to lose its natural color. Patches of lighter skin appear. Some people develop a few patches. Others lose much more skin color. A section of hair can turn white. Some people lose color inside their mouths. Even an eye can lose some of its color.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Depigmentation of skin, hair (scalp, eyebrow, eyelash, beard), eyes, mouth (inside) and genitals
- A few people say that the skin affected by vitiligo itches or feels painful

Treatment:
- Non medical treatment (use cosmetics to camouflage lost color)
- Potent or super-potent corticosteroid
- Non steroidal anti-inflammatory (Tacrolimus)
- Light treatment
- Photochemotherapy (PUVA)
- Surgery
- Depigmentation

Recommended products: Remus Ointment (Tacrolimus 0.03% & 0.1%)

28. Dry Skin (Xerosis)
========================================
Dry skin, medically known as Xerosis, is a common condition where the skin lacks adequate moisture in its outer layer (stratum corneum). This leads to roughness, scaling, and reduced skin elasticity. It can be caused by environmental factors (cold weather, low humidity), excessive washing, harsh soaps, aging, or underlying conditions like eczema or hypothyroidism.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Rough, tight feeling of the skin
- Flakiness or scaling
- Itching (pruritus)
- Cracks or fine lines on the skin
- Redness or irritation
- Dull, ashy appearance
- In severe cases: deep cracks (fissures) that may bleed

Treatment:
- Moisturizer
- Mild topical corticosteroids for itching or inflammation

Recommended products: Emolent Cream & Lotion (Light liquid paraffin & White soft paraffin), Emolent Plus Cream (Light Liquid Paraffin + White Soft Paraffin + Glycerin), Emoli Lotion (Benzalkonium Chloride + Chlorhexidine Hydrochloride + Isopropyl Myristate + Light Liquid Paraffin)

29. Sunburn
========================================
Sunburn is an acute inflammatory reaction of the skin caused by excessive exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, primarily UVB rays from the sun. It damages the outer layers of the skin and, in more severe cases, can affect deeper layers. Sunburn not only causes immediate discomfort but also increases the long-term risk of skin aging and skin cancer.

Signs & Symptoms:
- Redness of the skin (Erythema)
- Pain or tenderness
- Warm or hot feeling on the affected area
- Swelling (Edema)
- Blister formation in severe cases
- Dryness and peeling of skin after a few days
- Itching

Treatment:
- Broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) when going outside
- Moisturizer to reduce dryness and peeling

Recommended products: Sunmask Cream (Zinc Oxide, Avobenzone, Octinoxate & Enzacamene)

30. Hirsutism
========================================
Hirsutism refers specifically to male-pattern hair growth in females. It is a condition where women develop excessive, coarse, dark hair in areas typically seen in men—such as the face, chest, back, and abdomen. It's usually linked to increased levels or sensitivity to androgens (male hormones like testosterone).

Signs & Symptoms:
- Excess facial hair (upper lip, chin, sideburns)
- Hair on chest, abdomen, lower back, or thighs
- Thick, coarse (terminal) hair instead of fine (vellus) hair
- Acne or oily skin
- Irregular menstrual cycles
- Scalp hair thinning (male-pattern baldness)
- Deepening of voice (in severe cases)
- Increased muscle mass or reduced breast size (rare, severe androgen excess)

Treatment:
- Oral contraceptive pills – regulate hormones and reduce androgen production
- Anti-androgens (e.g., Spironolactone) – block effects of male hormones
- Topical creams like Eflornithine – slow facial hair growth

Recommended products: Elorim Cream (Eflornithine Hydrochloride 13.9%)
